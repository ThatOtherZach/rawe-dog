## Project Impact
> *Can you describe one of the most successful Salesforce implementations you led or supported during your time at Industrious? What made it successful, and what role did you play?*

We deployed Salesforce for OMA (Ontario Medical Association) which involved numerous departments and stakeholders to ensure a successful deployment. One of the big pain points in the deployment was testing and user feedback. Given the size of the org and the number of stakeholders required for sign off, the first attempt resulted in a collection of excel files and emails that was highly time intensive and difficult to prioritize tasks. 

The solution, was a **custom Salesforce app** that was installed in the **UAT** (User Acceptance Testing) instance which allowed users to submit and tag feedback and issues within Salesforce. These feedback records were only accessible to management stakeholders and the implementation team. **This allowed the team at Industrious CRM to prioritize tasks** and table items for future implementations - and we even used default Salesforce reports to display feedback testing progress to management to show progress.

The application has since been upgraded and improved on to the point where **Industrious CRM still uses the "ZAP" feedback application in other deployments** for clients as the ability to collect and manage issues in a deployment was made much easier to manage and built within a clients Salesforce org allowed the tool to be **adopted internally**.

My role in the development of the app was as **its chief developer and designer.** I leveraged **custom and standard Salesforce functionality** to reduce installation risk as well as leveraged a custom **Lightning Web Component** that showed progress on a record to the user. Development included working with **Apex** code, **HTML**, **CSS**, as well as **JavaScript** with integrated **SOQL (Salesforce’s version of SQL)** queries. **I custom designed the page layouts** and **list views for maximum impact** all while keeping each role, page, and field organized into Salesforce permission sets for **easy management and access** by end users on the implementation team and on the client side as well. 

Overall my work on the ZAP app had **lead to a successful implementation** at OMA as well as provided an additional tool for the team at Industrious CRM to **leverage for other clients** - thereby increasing their standing as an implementation partner and **providing additional end benefit** to the prospective client.

## User Feedback Loop
> *You created a feedback-gathering app in Salesforce. What inspired that? How did it function, and what impact did it have?*

Gathering feedback is a critical part of testing and often one that many users do not look forward to. For many other projects we would use **Microsoft Excel**, but this was not ideal for multiple users. The online solutions like a **Google Form** was also less idea as it still produced a poorly formatted and hard to use a **CSV** that would ultimately get edited to no end as we sorted through issues. This was a data collection problem that required specific formatting and an elegant design that **would work with users, not against them**.

The **ZAP App is very basic** at its core, its a simple feedback record paired with a comments record in Salesforce. Using Salesforce functionality like **Quick Actions** and **custom page layouts** I was able to integrate the app into Salesforce. From there it I was able to build **custom fields** which were controlled by **permission sets** so that some information that we used internally could be appended to the feedback record. This made the application as much a part of the user's life as it did ours, we could **collaboratively resolve feedback or issues** without leaving the Salesforce environment! 

Perhaps my proudest moment in building the application was in creating the **Lighting Web Component** (**LWC**). Viewing and making comments on the record using a standard list view was great to start, but overtime became a hassle and information was lost simply because reading comments was a challenge. **I coded my own comments feed** which mimicked the standard Salesforce Activities component. This required all my knowledge of **HTML**, **CSS**, and **knowledge** as well as a crash course in **Apex** and **SOQL (Salesforce’s version of SQL)** queries so I could grab the correct records from the Salesforce object to show for the correct feedback item. I learned a lot about **Salesforce devops** in this process which allowed me to **communicate easily with developers** - thus bringing the team closer to together to solve issues. For the impact see the section on the **ZAP application** in the **Project Impact** section.

## Technical Creativity
> *You used HTML/CSS and Lightning Design System — what’s one UI you’re most proud of designing? What problem did it solve, and how did users react?*

In the **ZAP application** there was a comments element, initially we used the default UI as that was easy. However in our initial tests we found that the way information was displayed wasn't ideal - so I built out a **custom Lighting Web Component** (**LWC**) using the **Lighting Design System** (**LDS**) to build out a new element which functioned very similar to a comments thread seen elsewhere on the internet. To build the element required knowledge of **HTML**, **CSS**, **JavaScript**, as well as **Apex** and **SOQL (Salesforce’s version of SQL)** to full implement and **run natively in the Salesforce environment**. Users, internal and external, loved the way the comments were displayed after this change as it allowed them to easily read the context of the issue, any other items that came up, and other information all without leaving the record page or opening the comment to read. This **enhanced** **user satisfaction** during UAT and **reduced back-and-forth communications** by consolidating context directly on the feedback record in Salesforce.

When it came to **data manipulation** for **migration** or **integration**, I was always trying my best to ensure that the systems we were building were **using the correct fields for the correct data type** - a skill that would later come in handy when I was able to get my **Salesforce Data Architect certification**. Using **Microsoft Excel** and **Google Sheets** was my main tools of choice and then I would use **Salesforce Data Loader** to push the data into Salesforce for testing and in some cases, **updating production** data. Everything in Salesforce is driven by good data, so with that in mind I would always try to make sure that users were given the ability to enrich the data in the most correct way from day one of using the system.

## Challenges Overcome
> *Was there a time something went wrong during a project? Maybe scope creep, resistance from users, or a technical hurdle? How did you handle it?*

Scope creep did occur at times across a few projects and this was primarily driven by the often overlooked flaw of Salesforce - while you can do it, doesn't mean it should do it. **Good communication** with the end client resolves a lot of this as well as having a great project manager to **keep expectations at a level that the team can meet**. While I didn't have to deal with this directly, it was a constant thing I kept an eye out for as we built.

Resistance from users also builds into that **management of expectations**. Users often don't like change, therefor the best way to introduce new features is **with care and kindness** as well as understanding that while the new process seems scary, it will **save the users time and effort** which at the end of the day, means the world to them. 

Technical hurdles were often as well, this is due to **the limitations of Salesforce's standard functionality**. While we did implement **custom code solutions** to address some of them, this was after we identified which features were **critical to the clients success** and then managing that feature development so that it not only **met the clients requirements** but also was built in a way which **allowed for easy integration** into the system. Most of the time my interactions with scope creep, user issues, and technical limitations came when I was demoing to the client or in end user training sessions.

## Training & Enablement
> *You ran live demos and training — what was your approach to making those engaging and effective for non-technical users?*

While running training sessions and live demos my main objective for any information shown on screen was to introduce the user to the main features they'll be using to **establish a baseline of familiarity** with the system, followed by the logic we at industrious used to develop that feature or why it was there based on requirements.

At one point we had a client, **Genesis Motors Canada**, who had users based in Quebec. Since they spoke French we had to **build out a separate set of training materials** that was then translated and passed to the Quebec users. The URLs included also had to be in French as well as the main login screen for Salesforce. We successfully helped Genesis Motors get up and running with Salesforce even with the additional hurdle of having to completely redo training materials for a small set of French users.

Lastly when I'm doing my demos I always check in with my audience from time to time to ensure that they're following along and understand the material presented. With Genesis Motors **I had to run several demos per day** for a week as there were **multiple dealerships across the country** and while each one was more or less the same material, I still took the time to **get to know the team** before getting started and made sure to address, flag, and report any dealership specific issue back to the team at Industrious so we could **work on a resolution as quickly as possible.**
