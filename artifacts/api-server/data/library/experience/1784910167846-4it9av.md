# Lane Four Work Experience Summary
####### **Note:** *This document is a collection of **my work experience at Lane Four from May 2021 to December 2021.** The questions are behavioral-style questions (in block quotes and italics; the bold items are keywords) which seek to pull more context out of my work experience so that my resume can be rewritten to fit a specific role/job posting that best highlights my unique workplace experiences.*
## Company Summary
**Lane Four is a Salesforce application development and implementation firm** based out of Toronto, Canada. The **mid-sized company** specializes in **Salesforce platform management and custom development** as well as manages a **Salesforce app** **called** "**Lane Four Highroad**". The Salesforce application allows users in Salesforce to **dynamically assign leads and clients** based on criteria from the record, this allows users in Salesforce to take their lead assignment to the next level with **complex routing** and assignment. **My term at Lane Four was short** as the rise of the COVID-19 Pandemic pushed me to look for other opportunities in the Salesforce space - however my main experience from Lane Four was working in a **QA** (**Quality Assurance**) environment and testing a **production ready application** in Salesforce.
## Skills, Tools & Technology
Lane Four primarily used a task management application for team assignment and project tracking. This was more or less pretty standard to what I have seen across the industry. The **Lane Four app "Lane Four Highroad"** was developed and tracked using **Jira**, which is also an industry standard for developing software. Other office software included the **Google Suite of apps** (**Google Docs**, **Sheets**, **Slides**, etc.) which was used for basic office admin documentation and client presentation materials. I spent most of my personal time in Jira and in the task management application which ensured that all the other members of the team was **kept up to date on the development tasks** and QA's current progress with new features. A lot of the end work for this workplace experience took place on the **Salesforce Platform** as that was what I was most certified to work on at the time.

## Process & QA
> *Can you describe a **QA process** or **client-reported bug** that you were responsible for testing? What tools did you use, and how did you ensure the issue was resolved correctly?*

While there were little to **no severe client bugs during my term** at Lane Four, we did test a number of features for the application which more or less boiled down to **input testing** and **record management**. Each time we added a new field we needed to test the field's ability to process whatever text a user may input. This required cycling though a number of **character combinations** using **test records and assignments** to verify that the logic and the field did not result in any adverse behavior.

For my testing I relied on **Google Sheets** and a **CSV** with some common inputs one would expect to fill a field with; for example, when using states for region tracking **I would test as many short and long forms** of the state as an input to verify that the functionality was working. In another occurrence we were looking for "free email" domains to exclude in the field, this required some research and another custom table of values to test against.
## Technical Communication
> *What kind of **technical documentation** or client-facing materials did you create while working at Lane Four? How did you make sure it was clear and usable for non-technical audiences?*

When a **new feature** was deployed for a client (after they requested it), I would always **include a brief summary** of the feature we implemented, the **logic/reason as to why** that feature was requested and built, and any **test cases of note** that came up in the **QA** (**Quality Assurance**) **process** and finally a **brief user guide** on how to use the feature should it need **a formal introduction for the end users**. My process for creating this documentation was **always written for users who were familiar with Salesforce** at a very basic level so that anyone with a cursory **knowledge of Salesforce** or the **development process** would be able to understand what was built and deployed into their Salesforce instance.

On occasion **when there was an error or unexpected test result**, I would include as much **technical detail** and **testing information** that I could gather before **creating an issue in Jira** and **assigning it to a developer**. On occasion the developer would need additional information which at the time (**COVID-19 Pandemic**) required a **Zoom call** **to discuss the issue** and navigate to a possible solution.
## Stakeholder Management
> *You attended regular **stakeholder meetings** at Lane Four — can you tell me about a time you helped translate business requirements into actionable system updates?*

Every day at Lane Four would begin with a **team sync meeting** and task assignment with a **review of the current development pipeline** as well as any other issues that could be on the horizon. These meetings **kept the internal team up to date** as well as **provided context for the developers** working on projects. Occasionally we would need additional information from a client regarding a feature they requested or an issue with the **Lane Four Highroad application**.

When this happened I, if assigned to tackle the issue, would then contact the client or user regarding the issue and **setup a call to discuss**. On those calls, I would **take notes** and then relay that information into an **email to the related party** and or **create an issue in Jira** that would track the progress on the item requested. **Solution design was a collaborative effort** at Lane Four and the Jira issue would be updated as the solution was scoped and built. Some of these details would be **passed back to the client for confirmation** and then **more secure business requirements** could be scoped out. Eventually, this would lead to a finished feature in the application or in the clients Salesforce instance.
## Platform Optimization 
> *Was there a time you identified an **in-org optimization** or configuration issue in a client’s Salesforce instance? What did you change, and what was the result?*

On occasion I would recommend that **clients look into simpler ways of organizing** their **Salesforce fields and page layouts** so that they would be more **friendly to end users**. The same advice was often conveyed to clients regarding **Salesforce list views, reports and dashboard** **management**. The result of this was two fold, I was able to **convey platform knowledge** to the client and enhance their perception of the Lane Four brand as well as **improve their perception of Salesforce** and how the platform can be **customized fairly easily** which can lead to **big changes in user productivity**.

**For example;** cited earlier in this document was a list of free email domains to be excluded from leads. This was due to a client finding that they were getting a lot of "junk leads" that often shared a similar domain name. The ask was to ensure that leads with those domains would be excluded from the assignment process and placed in a separate bucket. We also used a **Salesforce Lightning Flow** to check when the lead record was created and if it was more than 30 days ago and had no follow up activity and was not assigned to a user (tracked in a separate fields) the lead record would be deleted. **The result was cleaner data** the client would work with and **more focus on valuable leads** in their sales process.

**Another example;** a client had used many **Salesforce Processes** (**Process Builder**) to manage records. While this was the correct way to do things in Salesforce at the time, **Salesforce Lightning Flow** has now become the standard for **workflow automation** in Salesforce. This meant that the client's **workflows** in **Process Builder** needed to be **documented and then recreated and tested** in **Salesforce Lightning Flow** instead. Lightning Flow is much better at processing records than Process Builder, however the flows were very complicated and required a lot of **testing and requirement management** to get right. The end result was a **successful migration away from Process Builder** and implementation of **Salesforce Lightning Flow** instead; which meant the client's Salesforce instance was **kept up to date on current Salesforce best practices**, the flows were faster than process builder, and the **requirements and documentation was provided** so that any and all future changes could be **easily managed by a Salesforce administrator** without too much background knowledge. 
## Cross-Functional Collaboration
> *Who did you work with most frequently (e.g., developers, project managers, client admins), and how did you ensure smooth **cross-functional collaboration** or **hand-offs** during a project?*

My day to day at Lane Four was **primarily meeting with internal stakeholders** such as **project managers**, **client reps**, and **developers**. My documentation and other materials would be passed to the end client only when the client rep deemed it necessary. Because my work involved so **many different parties**, I would always make sure **my documentation was clear and concise**. I would often have my own **dictionary of terms if required** for non-Salesforce users as well as **included screenshots with annotations** if needed. For internal documentation, the structure was the same and the only major difference was that my **testing data was included and annotated** to the best of my ability. 
## Learning & Adaptability
> *Was there a moment you had to get up to speed quickly on a **new org, client need, or unfamiliar feature**? How did you approach the learning curve?*

This was basically everyday. Lane Four had **a very diverse client list** and clients were passed around based on the skill set required to **complete the business requirements successfully**. When I needed assistance internally I would first rely on my manager (client rep) for assistance, they would sometimes point me in the direction of another Lane Four member for assistance depending on the task at hand.

**I am a strong believer in team dynamics and collaboration** as no one person can be a full expert when it comes to Salesforce. Ultimately we all need help at one time or anther and **I rely on my teammates as much as they rely on me** when either of us needs help. When that avenue failed, I would rely on my existing **knowledge of the Salesforce Platform** to determine a solution. I pride myself on being a **quick learner who works well independently** and my term at Lane Four was no different to that end.
