# Sprachcaffe GEOS Languages Plus Work Experience Summary
####### **Note:** *This document is a collection of **my work experience at** Sprachcaffe GEOS Languages Plus** from **March 2015 to April 2017**. The questions are behavioral-style questions (in block quotes and italics; the bold items are keywords) which seek to pull more context out of my work experience so that my resume can be rewritten to fit a specific role/job posting that best highlights my unique workplace experiences.*
## Company Summary
This company no longer exists to the same extent it used to. At the time I worked for the company they had **over 30 language schools across several countries** as well as a vacation destination tour packages. It was more of a travel booking experience company with a language school attached and while I did spend most of my time **managing the language school's social media and website content**, there was some overlap into the other products and services the company offered. I was ultimately let go from the company due to budgeting restrictions and the company rapidly declined financially in the years leading up to the COVID-19 Pandemic.
## Skills, Tools & Technology
My main role and responsibility was concerned with copy writing and social media with some light **Google Ad Words management** work included. As a recent marketing graduate at the time, this was my first workplace experience and I was excited to lend my abilities to get others excited about learning a language abroad. 

My day to day tools included the **Adobe Creative Suite of apps** (**Photoshop** and **Illustrator**) for content creation on social media; later this would expand to include free image manipulation tools such as **Canva**. We used **Microsoft Office** for all internal communication tools such as **Microsoft Word**, **Powerpoint**, and of course **Outlook**. When it came to web apps I was given control of **Sprout Social** credentials which allowed us to easily post and monitor content on social media. There were also a handful of times **I had to use a custom built booking app on a server**, which required a high degree of knowledge to use as the system was very old and somewhat temperamental. Thankfully I was able to **learn on the fly with minimal help** from my manager who also knew very little about the software.

## Campaign Success
> *Can you tell me about a campaign or piece of content you created that performed especially well? What was the goal, and how did you measure success?*

At the time, Instagram content was a new space to try and produce content for. I was in-charge of managing that as well as the many (at least seven) social media **accounts across three platforms** (**Instagram, Facebook, Website**). The main way to drive leads was pushing to a landing page via social media or an ad placement and then the content on that page (also managed by our team) would drive the reader to fill out a form which was the lead capture moment. 

On one occasion **I designed a custom postcard** that featured the Toronto school and some of the attractions nearby and we printed a few copies to hand out to students. Since I was also the one writing most if not all the social media and landing page content that meant that a lot of the students would have come into contact with something I had written before they ultimately signed up for the language course. I always focused on the destination first, then the ability to learn a language. Since the students were interested in learning a language there didn't need to be that much "pressure" there so one could focus on where the user wanted to learn instead.

**I personally love to travel** and I tried to breathe that passion into my writing and focus in on stuff the student can see or experience at the school in addition to the other programs Sprachcaffe GEOS offered such as home-stays where the student would live with a family deepening their language learning as well as giving them a unique experience in a far away land. I never had access to any raw numbers, but the Toronto school was cited as being one of the best.
## Social Media Strategy
> *How did you determine what type of content or tone to use for different social media profiles and audiences? Any platforms or formats you found worked best?*

Sprachcaffe GEOS's primary target audience was teenagers and young adults; knowing this and being a young adult at the time, I focused on selling the **travel aspect of the experience** and then the language learning aspect. With a **travel first perspective** this pushed us to focus on why learning French in Montreal was the best option do to its liberal values and French culture while also showcasing the Canadian side of our brand identity. The tone was **always friendly**, but professional paired with some emojis and a call to action to visit the website for more information. 

For the travel package side of the business, this tone had to change. The target audience there was older and more refined, it didn't matter where so much as what they could do once they go to the destination. For these posts we **focused on experiences** and **comfort along with safety** and how the whole tour from airport pickup to drop off was **professionally managed**. The imagery was also more pristine, we often focused on tropical destinations as the older demographic data we had put an emphasis on targeting them. 

I really liked using **Instagram** for sharing content as it allowed us to put the destination front and center with our call to action in the description. The profile page also functioned as a kind of stripped down **mobile website**, so we would create content which drew in users as they scrolled past other posts.
## Website & CMS
> *What kind of CMS did you work with? Were you also involved in SEO, A/B testing, or performance tracking of landing pages?*

Sprachcaffe GEOS was using **Typo3** as their **Content Management System** (**CMS**) and website. **Typo3** is notorious at being hard to use as well as severely limited, however I was able to leverage my knowledge of its internal framework, **HTML**, **CSS**, and some **Javascript** to make custom sections on the website to better convey our calls to action. I was able to create some **custom HTML snippets** that we could leverage across several pages, thus reducing effort on my side as well as for the **off-site development team**. I was also careful to watch and include as much **Search Engine Optimization** (**SEO**) in the web copy so that our pages and images were tagged correctly so that our pages could score higher in the search rankings. Some **A/B testing** was conducted, which helped us determine what content could be reused on some pages for maximum impact. The website activity was all tracked using **Google Analytics**, which helped us determine our traffic sources as well as what content we published on social media was driving the best results when it came to booking a course or travel package.

When I wasn't working on marketing copy for the website or social media I was also interacting with students and other employees at the school to **learn about their experiences** and how their studies were coming along. It was a **very small team internally**, basically just **three people in-charge of seven brands** and their respective accounts.

Most of my activities were centered on **digital marketing and content creation**. ## Ad Performance Optimization
> *You reported on AdWords metrics — were you also involved in running the campaigns or just in reporting? Can you share how you helped improve ROI or reach?*

My role with **Google Ad Words** at the time was to monitor the progress and budget of the existing ads and keywords and my instruction was to **only make changes when necessary**. The campaigns more or less were there for brand recognition more so than lead generation which was the primary focus of our efforts on social media. I would include **Google Ad Words** in my tool belt only to the extend that I know my way around the tool and can get help to understand some aspects better, however it is **not the primary objective of my employment or career**.
## Problem Solving or Adaptability
> *Was there a time you had to jump into a totally unfamiliar task or tool? How did you handle it?*

Basically everyday! Sprachcaffe GEOS was **my first "real job"** and I was more than able to **figure out things on my own** using the tools I had at my disposal. I learn best this way and **with the right guidance** I can be an expert in no time. For **Google Ad Words**, I strongly relied on YouTube **tutorials and guides** on how to use the tool effectively. When those guides wasn't enough I would **dive into the respective developer documentation**, for example for **Typo3** there were many online resources and code snippets I could leverage in my work to make the website better. I would also **create my own documentation** for some of the code snippets I created in **Typo3**, which **allowed my team to easily update the website** and the features I helped build if I was away from the office.

This was my first workplace experience and a lot of the self documentation of work, tasks, projects, etc. habits were built up in this single year of employment at Sprachcaffe GEOS. To this day I still keep in contact with some of my former coworkers.
## Language or International Focus
> *Did your work involve adapting content for different regions or languages? How did you manage cultural or linguistic differences in messaging?*

One of the limitations of **Typo3** was that it **didn't allow for much customization**. The solution was to build out separate pages in each language as deemed necessary. The result was we only had **four languages to support** for our **core customers**; **French**, **German**, **English**, and **Mandarin Chinese**. Because of my childhood in Canada, as a Canadian citizen I had some exposure to French. I was also able to make sense of German from time to time and I was always able to ask another employee (who was **German**) for help, the same for **Mandarin Chinese**. 

For cultural and linguistic differences, there wasn't much I could do for the **English** content, however for **SEO** (**Search Engine Optimization**) I would leverage simple language and keywords as best as I could so that the content, when translated, would appear in the results. On occasion I would also ask the students at the school if they had any places or experiences I could include which may not be as widely known about. This **added a personal touch** to the website content and **helped build credibility** of the brand.

**I really enjoyed my time at Sprachcaffe GEOS** and it was where a lot of my **core workplace habits were formed** and sharpened. This was an early point in my career, but that was unfortunately cut short by budget restrictions. As I mentioned, I still keep in touch with former coworkers which shows how strong our workplace relationship was when I left the position.
