# TD Securities Work Experience Summary
####### **Note:** *This document is a collection of **my work experience at TD Securities from April 2022 to April 2024. **The questions are behavioral-style questions (in block quotes and italics; the bold items are keywords) which seek to pull more context out of my work experience so that my resume can be rewritten to fit a specific role/job posting that best highlights my unique workplace experiences.***
## Company Summary
**TD Securities** is a division of **TD Bank**, one of the **largest financial institutions in Canada**. My role at TD was as a **Business Systems Analyst IV** for the **Global Risk department** within TD Securities. As a **BSA** (**Business Systems Analyst**) my duties were to **take business requirements** from the Global Risk team and **create requirements** and then **develop features** for the **TD Salesforce platform**. This was **a contract position** for two years over which myself and my team **successfully deployed an application** (called **PIA**) to the Global Risk team. The application was a **streamlined version of an internal process** and document that would be used to determine the risk level for any data based project at TD.
## Skills, Tools & Technology
Due to the enterprise nature at TD, the **software stack was regimented**. I would conduct my daily tasks in **Microsoft Teams** and **Outlook** as well as the other **Microsoft Office** apps (**Word**, **PowerPoint**, **Excel**, **Notes**, etc.). When it came to development, **QA** (**Quality Assurance**), and **issue tracking** we used **Jira**. The **Salesforce platform** was of course in the background as well, which is where most of TD's core applications were based. I would on occasion use the **Salesforce Data Loader** for testing my own data in a **sandbox** org, but data uploads to production were to be conducted by a senior team member, usually one of the system architects.

## Process & Feature Delivery
> *Can you describe a time you were responsible for managing or coordinating a new **feature development**? How did you ensure it met the **business requirements** and fit into the release process?*

When I arrived at TD the "**PIA Project**" was still in its infancy. We had a rough schema and application in development, but many of the core features **the business** (**"the business"** is what we referred to **the internal team**) requested were not developed and or in their **very early stages of development**. My role as the **BSA** (**Business Systems Analyst**) was to **lead feature development** by having meetings with both the **business team stakeholders** and the developers to **ensure features and bugs/issues were being worked on** and completed in a timely manner. I would do this by **taking notes** and then **preparing my own documents** for the feature to be developed. I would then **create a Jira issue** (also called **User Stories**, **Epics**) that would track the progress on that item. Once the feature was marked as completed and the **QA** (**Quality Assurance**) team had given their approval, the feature would be pushed into production in the next release cycle, **typically every month** or so depending on the release notes.
## QA & Test Case Management
> *What was your approach to writing or managing **test cases** during your time at TD? How did you ensure quality and consistency during QA cycles?*

When **writing test cases** I would try to get as much information as I could from the **internal team** (**the business**) at TD. I would then **review those notes on my own**, **condensing the core functionality** into a set of bullet points that logically followed what the request was. I would the email the internal team with a **meeting follow up** on the feature to be developed along with those **notes outlining how the feature would work**. If the internal team signed off on these items, I would then write the **user story** and **test cases** to follow those requirements.

**For testing**, this was often **done by the** **QA team** who I had limited interactions with due to the organizational structure at TD. My role was to **review the tests completed** and, if necessary, retest and **provide clearer instructions** as to the expected income. On occasion test cases would fail, and this would be a result of **conflicting business logic** and or **improper test materials** the QA team was using (such as incorrect user permissions, browser cache, bad test data, etc.). When a test failed I would **review the screenshots and test data** the QA team provided and then **determine the solution** to that issue. 

**For example**, the **PIA Project** used a lot of **custom display logic**. The application had a **survey component** that would**&#32;dynamically display questions** based on previous replies to other questions. This logic used **basic operators** (**OR**, **NOT**, **IF**, etc.) and **custom ID's for questions** and their respective answers... In short these were **very complicated logic statements** that often were the source of many issues because of how long the survey was and how many questions and branches it had. I **worked with the QA team exclusively** on these issues and would often go back to the internal team (the business) to discuss the issue we were having and if there was perhaps **a better or more efficient way of answering the question** or changing the logic so that the question didn't rely on complex operators. **These efforts were successful** and eventually the logic for all **one thousand questions** and **four thousand answers** worked flawlessly.
## Communication & Change Management
> *How did you keep **stakeholders and internal teams** informed about project progress, system updates, or changes in requirements?*

We primarily use **Microsoft Teams for communication** with **standard emails** as well for tracking requirements and more **generalized project updates**. **Jira** was also used to **track developments** as feature and issue items would be linked to what we called "**Epics**" in **Jira**. These epics would then be assigned to a release when they finished the development cycle. While we did **create issues in Jira** for nearly all of the requests the internal team made, we would **prioritize these issues based on effort, scope, and of course resource availability** (developers, QA team, etc.). We would also **assign story points** on a scale of one to ten to determine and rank features and issues by the amount of effort. All of these tickets and issues **flowed into a central dashboard** which **tracked the overall project** and it was these metrics we would ultimately cite when I or my team updated the business on our progress.
## Documentation & Operational Support
> *What types of **project documentation** or **data sets** did you work with, and how were they used during and after system deployment?*

For project documentation, I would **create my own documents outlining the feature** to be developed and then when it came to **creating the issue in Jira** I would attach that document and any other details required for the feature development. This way **information was always shared** with my team while I was offline or away from my desk. The data the application used as well was stored across several **Excel** documents with rich formatting and headers so that the data could be exported into a **CSV** and **imported into Salesforce** using the **Salesforce Data Loader**.

The **upload process was complicated** as specific **Salesforce objects** (databases) needed to be **updated in order of their parent-child relationship** as this is how Salesforce links records together. **Parent ID mapping** was key and we kept **separate documents to ensure data was uploaded to the correct fields** using these mapping files. When these data sets were not being worked on or uploaded they were **centrally stored in a shared drive** so that anyone could access and update the data if needed.
## Cross-Functional Collaboration
> *Who did you work most closely with (e.g.**developers**, **QA**, **analysts**), and how did you ensure smooth **hand-offs or updates** across teams?*

For half of my tenure at TD I worked with another **BSA** (**Business Systems Analyst**) to maintain the project, however in the second half of my term **I would be the only BSA for the PIA Project** as the other **BSA** had left on maternity leave. My work with the **developers** and **QA (Quality Assurance) team** accelerated at that point as I was **more or less in charge of feature development** for the rest of my contract term. Thankfully, I had one year of **on the job training** and because **I pride myself on being a quick learner** I was able to pick up the baton and carry on with tasks easily. My documentation and communication over **Microsoft Teams** and **Outlook (email)** kept all **stakeholders up to date** on the project and feature development. When the time came to leave TD I had created a folder on the shared network drive with **all my working files for the project** as well as passing a zip file of these documents to my manager so that in the event of an issue after I am gone, they would have the files I was using on hand to assist.
## Learning & Problem Solving
> *Was there a time you had to adapt to an **unfamiliar tool**, **platform**, or **project scope**? How did you ramp up and contribute effectively?*

As mentioned previously the **PIA Project** had a **survey component** which was **built on several objects** each containing **thousands of individual records** whose only relation to other records was via a **unique ID**. At first these ID's were just an every increasing number, but as the project continued to develop it was clear this was not a scalable way to address the issue. To that end **I rebuilt the unique ID system** so that it was prefaced by a short hand text identifier and an incrementing number instead. The excel files themselves also **contained several filtering columns** so that one could filter out thousands of records based on those sections or other criteria allowing a user to **easily update and manage** the ever growing data set. I was essentially **learning how to structure data on the fly** with little knowledge of **Microsoft Excel**, nonetheless I was able to clean up the files and the **result was faster edits to the data sets** which in turn meant the survey could be **tested faster** and issues could be resolved faster. 

While I had some experience with **Jira** prior to this contract I was able to learn more about the tool and build up my own knowledge of how it fits in the **development cycle**. Grading features and issues based on effort became a learned skill as I had to discuss the objective with each team, cycle back to the internal business users for confirmation, and then again talk to the developers to figure out a solution. Overall this contract **gave me a lot of experience in developing enterprise software**.
