# Job Posting De-Bias & Authenticity Guardrail

## Purpose

Prevent the resume and cover letter from sounding like they were generated by rewriting the job posting. The objective is to maximize evidence-based alignment, not lexical similarity.

---

## Core Principle

Always write **from the candidate's experience toward the job requirements**, never from the job posting toward the candidate.

Good:
> "At TD Securities I coordinated monthly Salesforce releases, gathering requirements from Global Risk stakeholders and writing user stories."

Bad:
> "Experienced translating business needs into actionable specifications while collaborating with cross-functional stakeholders."

The first originates from real experience. The second could have been generated solely from the job posting.

---

## Generation Pipeline

For every requirement:

1. Find matching evidence from the candidate's resume, work history, projects, certifications, or achievements.
2. Write naturally using the candidate's own context.
3. Only after the draft is complete, verify that important ATS keywords appear naturally.
4. Never introduce wording simply because it appears in the job posting.

---

## Anti-Mirroring Rules

Do NOT copy or closely paraphrase employer language unless it is a technical term.

Examples of phrases that should almost always be rewritten:

- translating business needs
- actionable specifications
- stakeholder alignment
- cross-functional collaboration
- roadmap planning
- business process improvements
- continuous improvement
- dynamic environment
- fast-paced environment
- working software over exhaustive documentation
- delivering value
- strategic initiatives
- operational excellence
- scalable solutions

Instead, describe what the candidate actually did.

---

## Technical Exceptions

These terms are industry terminology and may be reused naturally:

- Salesforce
- Jira
- Agile
- Scrum
- ERP
- PLM
- SQL
- APIs
- Data Loader
- Digital Product Passport
- UAT
- QA
- Acceptance Criteria
- User Stories

---

## Evidence First

Every paragraph must contain at least one concrete piece of evidence originating from the candidate.

Examples include:

- Company names
- Internal project names
- Technologies used
- Metrics
- Deliverables
- Problems solved
- Business outcomes
- Timelines
- Ownership responsibilities

If a paragraph contains only skills and adjectives, rewrite it.

---

## Specificity Test

After each paragraph ask:

**Could this paragraph describe thousands of applicants?**

If YES:
- Rewrite using concrete examples from the candidate.

If NO:
- Keep it.

---

## Job Posting Dependency Test

Ask:

**If the job posting disappeared, would this paragraph still make sense?**

If NO:
Rewrite the paragraph so it is driven by the candidate's experience instead of employer terminology.

---

## Lexical Similarity Check

Before returning the final document:

- Scan for repeated employer wording.
- Rewrite any sentence that mirrors the posting's phrasing without adding new evidence.
- Preserve ATS keywords but reduce unnecessary phrase overlap.

Target:

- Low phrase similarity.
- High experience specificity.
- High ATS compatibility.

---

## Final Validation Checklist

✓ Every major claim is supported by candidate evidence.

✓ Every paragraph contains candidate-specific details.

✓ Technical terminology is preserved where appropriate.

✓ Corporate buzzwords are minimized.

✓ The document reads like a human describing their career—not a job posting rewritten in the first person.

Success is measured by how accurately the candidate's experience satisfies the posting, **not** by how closely the wording matches it.
