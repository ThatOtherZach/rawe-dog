# Writing Good Experience Files

The experience files in your `references/` folder are where the LLM gets its concrete material. Generic summaries produce generic output. Rich, specific files produce strong, believable documents.

## What Makes a Good Experience File

- **Specific projects** with real names or clear descriptions
- **Measurable outcomes** (even rough ones are better than none)
- **STAR-style stories** (Situation, Task, Action, Result)
- **Context** about the environment (team size, tools, constraints)
- **Your actual contribution** (not team achievements phrased as individual)

## Recommended Structure

Use the template in `templates/experience-file.md`. Focus on:

1. Role context (what kind of company/environment)
2. Key tools and skills used
3. 2–4 strong projects or themes with impact
4. Any notable process improvements or wins

## Tips

- Write in your natural voice. The LLM will match your tone.
- Include project names when they exist (ZAP app, internal tooling, client migration, etc.).
- Quantify where possible: "reduced feedback processing time by ~60%", "supported rollout to 8 dealerships", "migrated 40,000+ records with <1% error rate".
- For independent or side work, still write it professionally ("the client needed...", "delivered X for Y").

The richer these files are, the less the LLM has to guess — and the less likely it is to hallucinate.
